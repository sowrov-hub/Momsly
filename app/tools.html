Sleep Training Guide

Most pediatric sleep guidance suggests waiting until at least 4-6 months before sleep training, since younger babies still need overnight feeds. Always check with your pediatrician first, especially if your baby was premature or has any health or feeding concerns.

Before you start

Tips for success

  • Keep a consistent bedtime routine (bath, book, bed) so your baby recognizes what's coming.
  • Put your baby down drowsy but still awake whenever possible — that's the moment self-soothing gets practiced.
  • Pick one method and one consistent response, and give it a full week before judging whether it's working.
  • Make sure the sleep space follows safe sleep basics: firm flat mattress, no loose blankets or soft toys, baby on their back.
  • If nothing improves after a week, or something feels wrong, stop and check in with your pediatrician or a sleep consultant.

Baby Exercise Guide

Always supervise physical activity, stop if your baby seems tired or upset, and check with your pediatrician if you have any concerns about their movement or muscle tone.
